    About & sources

    About this dashboard

    Macro Monitor collects official statistics for the United States, the United Kingdom and the euro area, explains what each one measures, and shows when the next figures are due. It also tracks national elections worldwide.

    How to use it

    • Overview: choose an economy on the left. Each card shows the latest figure, the change since the previous one and a 3-year trend line. Click a card for the full history, a data table and an explainer.
    • Upcoming releases (right of the Overview): click any release to see what it measures, why it matters and the current figures.
    • Learning Centre: a 10-minute economics explainer and a country spotlight every morning, with an email option.
    • Compare: put the same statistic for several economies on one chart, or build your own.
    • Search: press / or Ctrl K anywhere to find a statistic, release or election.

    Reading the figures

    % y/y
    Change on the same period a year earlier. Removes seasonal patterns; the usual way to quote inflation.
    % m/m, % q/q
    Change on the previous month or quarter. More timely, but noisier.
    Annualised
    A quarterly change scaled up to an annual rate (roughly ×4). US GDP is usually quoted this way, so it looks larger than UK or euro area GDP growth.
    pp
    Percentage points: the difference between two percentages (3.5% to 3.9% is +0.4pp).
    Seasonally adjusted
    Corrected for regular calendar patterns (Christmas, summer). Where a series isn't, compare with the same period last year; the cards do this automatically.
    Revisions
    First estimates are often revised as more data arrives, especially GDP and migration. The charts always show the latest vintage.

    Updates

    Data is refreshed automatically on weekdays at 07:30, 15:15 and 22:00 UK time (UTC), shortly after the main UK and US release times. Figures can lag the official release by a few hours. If a source can't be reached, the last good figure is kept and marked Stale.

    Caveats

    This is an independent personal project, not an official statistics service, and nothing here is investment advice. Always check the source before relying on a number. The election calendar comes from volunteer-edited Wikipedia pages, so snap elections appear only once editors add them.
